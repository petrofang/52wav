About the numbers

The list and summit elevations come from the Over the Hill Hikers (June 2025 revision, 2019 statewide LIDAR). Trail distances, climbing and effort ratings are from NH Family Hikes, cross-checked against New England Waterfalls; anything marked est. was measured from OpenStreetMap trails and USGS elevation data instead. Coordinates come from OpenStreetMap, towns and counties from the US Census, and landowners from NH GRANIT. Peak names are cross-checked against the federal USGS GNIS gazetteer; where the hiking name differs from the official one, the list's name is shown. A * on a view score means nobody has rated it yet, so it shows the list median.

Your ticks stay yours. There is no account and no tracking — checkmarks live in this browser only. Use Save my progress to turn them into a link you can bookmark or email yourself, which is the safest way to carry a multi-year list between phones and computers. The checklist prints on a single page; choose Save as PDF in your print dialog if you would rather keep a copy on your computer. When you finish, send it to the Over the Hill Hikers for your patch.

Please take the data. Everything behind this page — summits, trailheads, routes, towns, landowners and federal name ids — is one plain JSON file, the same one your browser just loaded. Use it for your own map, app or spreadsheet under the Open Database License, which asks only that you credit the sources above and pass the same freedom along. Help yourself to peaks.json on GitHub or its public Gist mirror, which updates automatically. Found a wrong turn or a stale trailhead? Tell us — corrections are the most useful thing you can send.
